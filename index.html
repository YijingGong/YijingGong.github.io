Where this is going

Measure, decide, evaluate, deliver

Better phenotypes feed better decisions. Simulation shows the long-term consequences of those decisions, and AI infrastructure gets the results to the people who make them.

  1. Measure

    phenotyping and genetic evaluation

    Derive health and resilience phenotypes from records farms already keep, and develop sensor- and vision-based phenotyping for traits that are hard to measure.

  2. Decide

    deep reinforcement learning

    Bring genetic merit and semen allocation into the replacement decision, so one policy handles which cows to keep and how to breed them.

  3. Evaluate

    whole-farm simulation

    Extend the RuFaS genetic module to fertility, health and longevity traits, and test what a selection strategy does to a farm over generations.

  4. Deliver

    LLMs and Extension

    Build a web-based AI decision-support tool for producers, and benchmark how accurate and reliable its readings of herd reports are.

About

AI for animal and dairy systems

Animal agriculture now has genetic evaluations, routine health and production records, and sensor data at an unprecedented scale. My research asks how to use that information well: how to measure animals better, how to turn data into breeding and management decisions, and what those decisions mean for profitability, animal performance, and the environment over time.

I am a Postdoctoral Research Associate with Victor E. Cabrera in the Department of Animal & Dairy Sciences at UW–Madison. My work combines deep reinforcement learning, quantitative genetics, mathematical optimization, whole-farm simulation, and large language models. I lead research on AI-driven cow replacement decisions, lead a Dairy Management Inc.–funded agentic AI project, and have led development of the genetic simulation module in the open-source Ruminant Farm Systems (RuFaS) model. As a research intern at the Council on Dairy Cattle Breeding (CDCB), I studied genetic evaluation of clinical mastitis in U.S. Holsteins.

My training spans three fields: a Ph.D. in Animal & Dairy Sciences (2025) and M.S. degrees in Computer Science and Food Science (2021) from UW–Madison, and a B.S. in Food Science from Cornell University (2017). I work primarily with dairy cattle and am interested in extending these methods to other species and production systems. I care about tools that are transparent, reusable, and useful to researchers, breeding organizations, Extension educators, and producers.
